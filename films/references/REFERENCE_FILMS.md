# Reference films (Day One Films): what each one does

Study material only. Learn the structure, never copy a story, device, line, location or character.
Contact sheets (one frame every 3 s, timestamped) are in `references/films/`.

## READY (2:40) `ready_*.jpg`
- Pain: waiting to feel ready before starting (guitar, open mic).
- Opening card: "He was going to start. / The moment he felt ready." A READY bar appears at 0%.
- Device: the READY % bar, top left, all film. Lessons, tips videos, gear and a checklist push it up: 12%, 45%, 78%, 99%. It stalls at 99% with a spinner. "It was never going to reach 100."
- Villain lines: "they'll all see you fail", "you're not ready", "just one more video", "they'll laugh", "go home".
- Teaching moment: a drawn graph. "Watching teaches you fast at first. Then it stops. Doing is embarrassing at first. Then it teaches you everything."
- Turn: he plays badly at home, then in the park, then on the street. A practice count, top left, climbs (31, 96, 170, 256). "Nobody was watching as hard as he feared."
- Climax: on stage the villain fills the room, he keeps playing, a warm white flash wipes it away.
- Mirror ending: a kid in a red cap watches through the window, then goes in himself. "He was never ready. / He went anyway."
- Title: READY / "You'll never feel ready." / "Start anyway." (red)

## STOIC: How One Old Book Fixed His Life (2:40) `book_*.jpg`
- Pain: rage at things outside your control (missed bus, rejection emails, rude people).
- Opening card: "Nothing ever went his way."
- Device: a heart-rate readout, top left (104, 146, 204 bpm). It climbs with every bad event and falls to 60 after the turn.
- Villain lines: "why is it always you?", "make them pay", "everyone is against you", "WHY ME?!", "you can't control anything".
- Rock bottom: alone under a streetlamp in the rain. He finds an old book (Meditations) that glows warm.
- Teaching moment: the circle of control drawn around him. Inside: my mind, my choices, my actions, my response. Outside: the weather, other people, the bus, the past, luck. "Some things are up to us. Some things are not." (Epictetus) "LET GO OF THE REST."
- Same world, different man: the first-act events replay unchanged. "The bus still left." "The answer was still no." "People were still people." He is calm this time.
- Practice montage, labelled bottom left: JOURNAL, READ, MEMENTO MORI (hourglass), DISCOMFORT.
- Climax: a storm and the villain hit the circle; it holds. A sunrise on the rock: "Be like the rock the waves keep crashing against. It stands firm, and the sea falls still around it." (Marcus Aurelius)
- Title: STOIC / "You can't control the storm." / "Only how you stand in it." (red). The title word is not the video title.

## COMFORT ZONE: You're Not Lazy. You're Too Comfortable. (3:32) `comfort_*.jpg`
- Pain: saying no until your world shrinks.
- Opening: a happy montage, not a black card. "He used to go everywhere with his friends." "And he always went first." Friends wear coloured beanies.
- Device: a map of his comfort zone, a glowing blob linking school, gym, Max's, skate park and bus stop. "Every time he said no, it got smaller." It shrinks to his bedroom, then to a ring around his bed.
- Villain lines: "they'll laugh at you", "she'll think you're weird", "everyone's better than you", "stay safe in here", "you'll fall", "go home".
- Turn: "He'd drawn every line himself." He picks up the chalk. "So he could step over them."
- Same world, different man: gym ("nice, man!"), class ("great job!"), the party, the market, the skate park, each one a yes now.
- Climax: the drop-in at the skate park, villain at full size, he drops in.
- Bookend: the map grows ("His world got bigger.") and the first shot replays: "He went first again."
- Title: COMFORT ZONE / "It's just a line you drew." / "Step over it." (red), then an end screen (like, subscribe, Skool link).

## OVERTHINKING: Stop Overthinking (4:06) `overthink_*.jpg`
- Pain: replaying every message and moment until you act on none of them.
- Opening: "He never did anything without thinking about it first." "Usually for days." A friend's unanswered texts.
- Device: red sticky notes. Each worry becomes a note ("is he mad?", "it's weird now", "what if it's wrong?", "they're laughing at you"). They spread across the wall and finally cover the room. Bad futures play in a red thought cloud.
- Villain: built out of the notes ("he'll laugh", "turn around", "what would you even say?").
- Turn: "Every note was a guess." He walks to the court. The notes turn white and fall.
- Payoff: four seconds of real talk: "thought you were mad at me." "He'd lived it a hundred times in his head. In real life, it took four seconds."
- Same world, different man: class, cafe and the post he never shared, each now done.
- Mirror ending: a younger kid in a red cap, "what if they say no?". He hands him one note.
- Title: OVERTHINKING / "You can't think your way to the answer." / "Go find out." (red)
- From the visual transcript (October 18): the hook plants the friendship with a photo, "me + sam", and a reminder card, "text sam". Sam's texts ("rematch tmrw", "u coming tonight?") sit unanswered while he rewrites his reply. Small, real triggers carry the worry: a "kinda lol" to Max becomes "is he mad?", a right answer in maths ("x = 6", "correct.") becomes "what did you do?", a drawing he never posts gets "nobody cares." The turn replays those same moments as evidence ("Every note was a guess.") and the reminder updates to "text sam back". The mirror ending rewrites the reminder one last time: "find out."
- What to take: the reminder card is a relabelled counter (text sam, text sam back, find out.), and the turn reuses the film's own earlier scenes as proof instead of a speech.

## LONELINESS: Loneliness | An Animated Short Film (2:31) `lonely_*.jpg`
- Pain: loneliness; days without speaking to anyone.
- Opening card: "WORDS SAID TODAY: 0", then "Some days he didn't say a single word." A phone feed of friends out together, a delivery at the door, friends in coloured beanies passing his window.
- Device: the WORDS SAID TODAY counter, top left, all film. 0 for days, 1 for "thanks" at the door.
- Villain lines: "nobody wants to hear from you", "see?", "don't.", "he's done with you".
- Low point: a birthday cupcake with one candle, alone. His phone rings, "hello?!", and it is an automated message from his phone provider ("Press one for an exclusive offer."). "It was the longest conversation he'd had in months."
- Turn: "He only had one life." He opens the door against the villain's "don't." Cafe ("a coffee, please"), then a run club where he meets Leo (orange beanie). The counter climbs: 10, 25, 54, 120, 353. "Every word got a little easier."
- Setback: Leo says "my parents are splitting up". He panics and answers "...cool?". Leo leaves, the counter drops back to 0, and the villain returns. Leo texts "you good? you coming? you ok?"
- Repair: Leo calls; he answers: "leo. i'm sorry." "i said cool. i panicked." The villain cracks with gold light. The counter runs to 1,204.
- Mirror ending: at the run club a quiet newcomer (green beanie) has his own "WORDS SAID TODAY: 0" and a small villain. He speaks to him first and the newcomer's counter goes to 1. "Someone just had to say the first word."
- No title card; the film ends on the Skool and like/subscribe end screens.
- Worth learning: the relationship nearly breaks after the turn (a second low point), and the repair is the real climax. The counter going up fast is a payoff on its own.

### 1 HOUR (3:14) `lastsoul_hour_*.jpg`, Last Soul, October 18 (150 views in its first 49 minutes)

### What to take from Last Soul
1. A helper character can deliver the turn with one simple, doable instruction ("Can you run one minute?"). The viewer leaves with something to try today.
2. Show the technique as steps on screen (a 1:00 timer, a three-line "how to stay calm" list) and let him practise them.
3. Put a relapse after the turn: the same excuse or trigger comes back, and this time he acts differently.
4. Weave the mirror into the plot: the person who copies him is in the story from the wound (TEMPER's son), not just added at the end.
5. Plant a small prop early that pays off as a bookend (the 14-DAY RETURNS sign, the shoe shop).
6. Feelings can be lit onto the character: an outline glow in the feeling's colour.
7. Inner excuses as small coloured captions beside the object that triggers them, instead of a villain body.
8. Quote cards on black with the author's name, two or three per film. Thin serif titles with a one-line subtitle.
9. Thumbnails: one bold word, high contrast, sometimes a white background (TEMPER) or a split before/after frame (1 MINUTE: grey and alone vs yellow and running).
10. Longer films (5:00 to 5:24) still hold, because something new happens every few seconds.

### FOCUS | An Animated Short Film (2:44, 8.4K views in 6 days) `focus_*.jpg` (with transcript)

### What to take from FOCUS
1. A moment that asks the viewer to act now, inside the film, with an on-screen timer. It turns watching into doing and is the most shareable beat.
2. Flip the villain's line at the climax so it means the opposite ("you can't stop" becomes "You can't stop now.").
3. One metaphor location for the fight (stairs, summit) instead of a literal room.
4. A villain inside a reflection (a dark screen, a window, a mirror).
5. A stinger after the title card that hints the fight goes on.
6. Villain lines placed like UI (notifications next to the object) rather than floating near the villain.

1. A mentor's own regret as the turn: a short, sad, true-sounding story is more convincing than any discovery.

4. The final act is a single text message ("Sorry, can it wait?"): an act every viewer can copy.
5. A bright white title card stands out after a dark film.

Overlap with our films: CONNECTED shares ground with LATER. (time with a loved one running out, a parent) and 1%. (work over the people you love). Our next films should not be about work versus family or a partner.

### What to take from NOT TODAY

## Patterns across all of them
1. First 5 seconds: one past-tense caption about "he" that states the pain ("He was going to start."). Or a happy before-shot that will be lost (COMFORT ZONE).
2. A device on screen from the first minute that measures the pain: a bar, bpm, a map, a calendar, notes. It gets worse in act 1, stalls at rock bottom, and changes meaning at the turn.
3. The villain is a red-glowing black silhouette. Its short lower-case lines are shown in red text and also whispered. It grows to full size at the climax and is wiped out by light.
4. The lesson is drawn, not said: a graph, a circle, a map, a quote with its author.
5. Act 1 sets up 3-5 places or moments so act 3 can replay them with the new behaviour ("The bus still left.").
6. A mirror ending: a younger kid in a red cap gets the lesson passed on, or the opening shot replays changed.
7. Title card: one or two words in caps with a red wavy underline, a quiet white truth, then a 2-3 word command in red.
8. Length 2:40 to 4:36. Chapters on YouTube use the beat names ("The window", "99", "The stage").
9. Colour is freer than our rule: warm exteriors (sunset park, orange track, sunrise), side characters in coloured beanies. Dark and muted until the turn, warmer after it.
10. Heavy topics end with a helpline card. Some films end with a like/subscribe/Skool end screen.
