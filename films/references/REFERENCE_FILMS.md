Study material only. Learn the structure, never copy a story, device, line, location or character.

## READY (2:40) `ready_*.jpg`
- Pain: waiting to feel ready before starting (guitar, open mic).
- Opening card: "He was going to start. / The moment he felt ready." A READY bar appears at 0%.
- Device: the READY % bar, top left, all film. Lessons, tips videos, gear and a checklist push it up: 12%, 45%, 78%, 99%. It stalls at 99% with a spinner. "It was never going to reach 100."
- Villain lines: "they'll all see you fail", "you're not ready", "just one more video", "they'll laugh", "go home".
- Teaching moment: a drawn graph. "Watching teaches you fast at first. Then it stops. Doing is embarrassing at first. Then it teaches you everything."
- Turn: he plays badly at home, then in the park, then on the street. A practice count, top left, climbs (31, 96, 170, 256). "Nobody was watching as hard as he feared."
- Climax: on stage the villain fills the room, he keeps playing, a warm white flash wipes it away.
- Mirror ending: a kid in a red cap watches through the window, then goes in himself. "He was never ready. / He went anyway."
- Title: READY / "You'll never feel ready." / "Start anyway." (red)

## STOIC: How One Old Book Fixed His Life (2:40) `book_*.jpg`
- Pain: rage at things outside your control (missed bus, rejection emails, rude people).
- Opening card: "Nothing ever went his way."
- Device: a heart-rate readout, top left (104, 146, 204 bpm). It climbs with every bad event and falls to 60 after the turn.
- Villain lines: "why is it always you?", "make them pay", "everyone is against you", "WHY ME?!", "you can't control anything".
- Rock bottom: alone under a streetlamp in the rain. He finds an old book (Meditations) that glows warm.
- Teaching moment: the circle of control drawn around him. Inside: my mind, my choices, my actions, my response. Outside: the weather, other people, the bus, the past, luck. "Some things are up to us. Some things are not." (Epictetus) "LET GO OF THE REST."
- Same world, different man: the first-act events replay unchanged. "The bus still left." "The answer was still no." "People were still people." He is calm this time.
- Practice montage, labelled bottom left: JOURNAL, READ, MEMENTO MORI (hourglass), DISCOMFORT.
- Climax: a storm and the villain hit the circle; it holds. A sunrise on the rock: "Be like the rock the waves keep crashing against. It stands firm, and the sea falls still around it." (Marcus Aurelius)
- Title: STOIC / "You can't control the storm." / "Only how you stand in it." (red). The title word is not the video title.

## COMFORT ZONE: You're Not Lazy. You're Too Comfortable. (3:32) `comfort_*.jpg`
- Pain: saying no until your world shrinks.
- Opening: a happy montage, not a black card. "He used to go everywhere with his friends." "And he always went first." Friends wear coloured beanies.
- Device: a map of his comfort zone, a glowing blob linking school, gym, Max's, skate park and bus stop. "Every time he said no, it got smaller." It shrinks to his bedroom, then to a ring around his bed.
- Villain lines: "they'll laugh at you", "she'll think you're weird", "everyone's better than you", "stay safe in here", "you'll fall", "go home".
- Turn: "He'd drawn every line himself." He picks up the chalk. "So he could step over them."
- Same world, different man: gym ("nice, man!"), class ("great job!"), the party, the market, the skate park, each one a yes now.
- Climax: the drop-in at the skate park, villain at full size, he drops in.
- Bookend: the map grows ("His world got bigger.") and the first shot replays: "He went first again."
- Title: COMFORT ZONE / "It's just a line you drew." / "Step over it." (red), then an end screen (like, subscribe, Skool link).

- Bookend: "It started with something real. / So did the way back."
- Title: ONE LAST TIME / "It's never the last time." / "Ask for help." (red), then a helpline card ("If you or someone you love is struggling, you are not alone. findahelpline.com").

## OVERTHINKING: Stop Overthinking (4:06) `overthink_*.jpg`
- Pain: replaying every message and moment until you act on none of them.
- Opening: "He never did anything without thinking about it first." "Usually for days." A friend's unanswered texts.
- Device: red sticky notes. Each worry becomes a note ("is he mad?", "it's weird now", "what if it's wrong?", "they're laughing at you"). They spread across the wall and finally cover the room. Bad futures play in a red thought cloud.
- Villain: built out of the notes ("he'll laugh", "turn around", "what would you even say?").
- Turn: "Every note was a guess." He walks to the court. The notes turn white and fall.
- Payoff: four seconds of real talk: "thought you were mad at me." "He'd lived it a hundred times in his head. In real life, it took four seconds."
- Same world, different man: class, cafe and the post he never shared, each now done.
- Mirror ending: a younger kid in a red cap, "what if they say no?". He hands him one note.
- Title: OVERTHINKING / "You can't think your way to the answer." / "Go find out." (red)

## LONELINESS: Loneliness | An Animated Short Film (2:31) `lonely_*.jpg`
- Pain: loneliness; days without speaking to anyone.
- Opening card: "WORDS SAID TODAY: 0", then "Some days he didn't say a single word." A phone feed of friends out together, a delivery at the door, friends in coloured beanies passing his window.
- Device: the WORDS SAID TODAY counter, top left, all film. 0 for days, 1 for "thanks" at the door.
- Villain lines: "nobody wants to hear from you", "see?", "don't.", "he's done with you".
- Low point: a birthday cupcake with one candle, alone. His phone rings, "hello?!", and it is an automated message from his phone provider ("Press one for an exclusive offer."). "It was the longest conversation he'd had in months."
- Turn: "He only had one life." He opens the door against the villain's "don't." Cafe ("a coffee, please"), then a run club where he meets Leo (orange beanie). The counter climbs: 10, 25, 54, 120, 353. "Every word got a little easier."
- Setback: Leo says "my parents are splitting up". He panics and answers "...cool?". Leo leaves, the counter drops back to 0, and the villain returns. Leo texts "you good? you coming? you ok?"
- Repair: Leo calls; he answers: "leo. i'm sorry." "i said cool. i panicked." The villain cracks with gold light. The counter runs to 1,204.
- Mirror ending: at the run club a quiet newcomer (green beanie) has his own "WORDS SAID TODAY: 0" and a small villain. He speaks to him first and the newcomer's counter goes to 1. "Someone just had to say the first word."
- No title card; the film ends on the Skool and like/subscribe end screens.
- Worth learning: the relationship nearly breaks after the turn (a second low point), and the repair is the real climax. The counter going up fast is a payoff on its own.

### 1 MINUTE (4:59) `lastsoul_minute_*.jpg` (with transcript: music, sighs and panting only)
- Pain: big plans that collapse ("10K. Every day.") and the shame of giving up.
- Opening card: "Big plans feel better than small steps." (serif, on black).
- Device: a wall plan "10K EVERY DAY" that fills with red crosses, and a 5:30 alarm with SNOOZE. Excuses are small orange captions next to the alarm: "Weather's awful." "Long week." "Tomorrow." "I'm just not a runner."
- Wound and stakes: he tries to return the running shoes; "14-DAY RETURNS" was planted on the counter at the start. "Sorry. It's been a month."
- Turn from a person, not a discovery: the shop worker (headband) says "That's the hardest part. Can you run one minute?" "Just one minute a day?" "Of course I can." "One minute. Anywhere." He tears the 10K plan down and writes "1 MIN".
- Practice shown concretely: the alarm reads "RUN 1 MIN", a 1:00 timer, short night runs, a watch reading 0.00 km. Quote cards on black with authors (Epictetus, James Clear).
- Relapse after the turn: the same excuses come back ("Weather's awful. Too cold. Tomorrow."), and he runs anyway. The first colour in the film is his yellow raincoat.
- Payoff: the plan wall fills with green ticks; then a bright green open hillside, the only daylight colour scene.
- Bookend: back at the same shop: "Hi. It's been a while. I need a new pair." "So you made it to 10K?" "I'm not chasing 10K anymore. Just one minute a day. Then I see where the run takes me."
- Title: 1 MINUTE / "Just start. See where it takes you." (thin serif caps, no underline).

### TEMPER (5:24) `lastsoul_temper_*.jpg` (no transcript)
- Pain: anger from work that he carries home ("Life is full of things that make us angry." / "Anger always finds its way home.").
- Device: a thermometer beside him (red when hot, blue when ashamed), and his body outline glows in the colour of his feeling (red, orange, blue).
- Wound: a delivery driver, a customer complaint, "DEDUCTED: CUSTOMER COMPLAINT" on his pay slip. At home he shouts "ENOUGH!" at his family; a red demon flashes behind him for one frame.
- Stakes inside the plot: his little son starts copying him (hits a classmate; a call from the school office). Quote card: "Children have never been very good at listening to their elders, but they have never failed to imitate them." (James Baldwin)
- Turn: he searches "how to stay calm": "Breathe for 10 seconds. Put on some music. Unclench your fists." He practises each one on screen (a sticky note on the dashboard, music in the van).
- Relapse: "Hard days will keep coming. You will still get angry." The same customer: "One more complaint and I'm fired." He counts his breath and goes calm.
- Payoff and mirror: his son smashes his toy rocket ("STUPID ROCKET!"); he says "Breathe with me", and the boy copies calm. They fix it together. "Children copy calm, too."
- Title: TEMPER / "Don't let it run your life."

### What to take from Last Soul
1. A helper character can deliver the turn with one simple, doable instruction ("Can you run one minute?"). The viewer leaves with something to try today.
2. Show the technique as steps on screen (a 1:00 timer, a three-line "how to stay calm" list) and let him practise them.
3. Put a relapse after the turn: the same excuse or trigger comes back, and this time he acts differently.
4. Weave the mirror into the plot: the person who copies him is in the story from the wound (TEMPER's son), not just added at the end.
5. Plant a small prop early that pays off as a bookend (the 14-DAY RETURNS sign, the shoe shop).
6. Feelings can be lit onto the character: an outline glow in the feeling's colour.
7. Inner excuses as small coloured captions beside the object that triggers them, instead of a villain body.
8. Quote cards on black with the author's name, two or three per film. Thin serif titles with a one-line subtitle.
9. Thumbnails: one bold word, high contrast, sometimes a white background (TEMPER) or a split before/after frame (1 MINUTE: grey and alone vs yellow and running).
10. Longer films (5:00 to 5:24) still hold, because something new happens every few seconds.

### FOCUS | An Animated Short Film (2:44, 8.4K views in 6 days) `focus_*.jpg` (with transcript)
- Pain: the phone eats the whole night ("It was never just one.").
- Device: a SCREEN TIME bar top-centre that fills red, and a clock top-right (2:47 AM, 2:58, 1:46). A wall PLAN he never starts, a calendar of red crosses.
- Villain: short red lines pinned next to the phone like notifications: "just one more." "check it." "you earned this." "start tomorrow." "everyone's online." "you're missing out." In the dark screen the villain appears in his own reflection; the screen cracks: "you can't stop. you never could."
- Rock bottom caption pair: "You don't lack time. / You give it away."
- Turn as one physical act: the clock reads 1:46, his hand turns the phone face down. Then "DAY 1." with a headband.
- Practice montage: running through the city at night, studying, push-ups with a counter (1, 4), then punching a red bag shaped like the villain.
- Metaphor scene: an endless dark staircase. "Distraction is easy." "Focus is a choice." The villain swoops in: "you can't stop." He keeps climbing, and the caption flips the villain's own line: "You can't stop now."
- Payoff: a summit at sunrise. "No one gives you your hours back. / So take them."
- New and strong: the film stops and talks to the viewer. "you're still holding it." (villain, to us) then "Flip your phone face-down." with a 10-second countdown ring (9, 7, 4, 2). The viewer does the act in real time.
- Title: FOCUS / "Look up. Your life is happening." / "Put it down." (red). Then a stinger after the title: a 5:00 alarm and "Five more minutes." (the next fight, and a nod to DAY 1.).
- Thumbnail: big bloodshot face in bed, cracked phone with the villain inside it, red 3:12 clock, "FOCUS." top right. Again the big-face layout: it works for the channel, but ours must vary (see STYLE_BIBLE).

### What to take from FOCUS
1. A moment that asks the viewer to act now, inside the film, with an on-screen timer. It turns watching into doing and is the most shareable beat.
2. Flip the villain's line at the climax so it means the opposite ("you can't stop" becomes "You can't stop now.").
3. One metaphor location for the fight (stairs, summit) instead of a literal room.
4. A villain inside a reflection (a dark screen, a window, a mirror).
5. A stinger after the title card that hints the fight goes on.
6. Villain lines placed like UI (notifications next to the object) rather than floating near the villain.

### CONNECTED | An Animated Short Film (5:33) `connected_*.jpg` (transcript: music only, a few words)
- Pain: always on for work, never present with the person beside you ("Slides by nine." "Did I miss something?" "Lie down with me?" "It's after ten.").
- Look: a different style from ours and Day One's. Solid black silhouette characters with white eyes and a thin rim light, softly blurred real-looking rooms, and dialogue as small subtitle lines, not bubbles. Mostly speech, almost no villain.
- Device: a stack of little notification counters floating by his head all film (Inbox 5, To-do 2, Messages 3), plus a status pill that reads "In a meeting", then "No connection", and finally a red-heart "Connected". The device is his availability.
- Wound and stakes inside the relationship: his partner asks "Can we go see my mom on Sunday?" He answers "...Circle back?". She writes "Dinner Thursday? Just us." in a red circle on the calendar, and adds it to his phone ("Added").
- Quote cards on black with authors: "While we are postponing, life speeds by." (Seneca); "Besides the noble art of getting things done, there is a nobler art of leaving things undone." (Lin Yutang).
- Turn from a mentor: an older boss (white hair, glasses) says "Nothing sits in my inbox for more than an hour." "Honestly? Never thought about it." "She kept asking to go back." He shows a photo of himself and his wife in Paris: "She passed away last year." "Now every morning I ask myself: Am I just busy? Or am I living?" (on a handwritten note). A sticky note on the frame: "Don't work late. Come home for dinner."
- Relapse and final fight: Thursday at the office, the anniversary reminder on his phone, "Need the new slides tonight", the question "Am I just busy? Or am I living?" comes back. He types "Sorry, can it wait?" and leaves with flowers.

### What to take from CONNECTED
1. A mentor's own regret as the turn: a short, sad, true-sounding story is more convincing than any discovery.
2. A small UI layer floating by the character (counters, a status pill) is a quiet device that never leaves the screen.
3. Planted props that pay off in another scene: the red circle on the calendar, the Paris photo, the sticky note that moves from his boss's frame to his own phone.
4. The final act is a single text message ("Sorry, can it wait?"): an act every viewer can copy.
5. A bright white title card stands out after a dark film.

Overlap with our films: CONNECTED shares ground with LATER. (time with a loved one running out, a parent) and 1%. (work over the people you love). Our next films should not be about work versus family or a partner.

### RESET: One Missed Day Didn't Ruin It. (2:45) `notToday_reset_*.jpg` (with transcript)

- Payoff: the friend asks him to count; the blocks lie gold across the grass: "Count them."
- Title: RESET / "A missed day is just a day." / "Never miss twice." (red), with the block tower drawn under it with one gap.

### What to take from NOT TODAY
1. The best idea in RESET: a second line on the same chart (DAYS DONE next to STREAK). The device is reread, not replaced.
2. The villain as a physical object holding him down (the blanket) is stronger than a figure standing nearby.
3. A stinger that names the next film turns single videos into a series. Use it once we have a run of films.
4. Do not copy the template. Their films blur together; ours must keep a new device, a new place and a new thumbnail layout every time.

### POV: You're a Dubai Prince (5:01), The Surreal Stories `surreal_dubai_*.jpg` (with transcript: music only)
A different genre: second-person captions, bright flat colours, comedy first, the lesson late. 1.2K views in 5 days on a small channel; its other POVs (internet shut off, 12-year-old billionaire) are the same format.
- Hook: "POV: YOU'RE A DUBAI PRINCE" title, then "And you've never paid for anything. Ever." ### What to take from these three
1. Timing is a lever. A topic the audience is searching for this month (winter arc in October, New Year in December, exams in spring) gets a running start. Plan at least one film a season around the calendar.
2. Show hidden progress. The best device here is the split screen: nothing visible above, a gold root growing below. The viewer sees what the hero can't, so they want him to keep going. Rereading the device at the payoff (every root branch is a night he studied) is the RESET lesson again.
3. A counter can end on a tiny number. The Dubai counter climbs to a billion and the payoff is the last $4. The surprise is in the smallest change, not the biggest.
4. One interactive beat ("comment A or B") lifts comments. Use at most one per film, at a real choice point, and only if it fits.
5. Second-person POV is an option for a light film: "you" puts the viewer in the seat. Our films stay third person ("he") unless a pitch needs it.
6. Highlight one word per caption in a colour, the word that carries the line.
7. Thumbnails seen here: a three-panel progression with DAY 1 / 15 / 30 tags and stat chips (USERS 138k, +$2,025 EARNED); a horror-lettered word in red over giant claws reaching for a tiny lit figure (PROCRASTINATION); a sad face holding up the proof (an F exam) under WHY 99% QUIT; bold two-line angled text in white and yellow with a thick outline and an arrow (The Surreal Stories). Day One's newest are all one or two words in white, ending in a full stop or question mark, over a single emotional pose.

### What to take from Monk Mode Films
1. A HUD that measures time since the habit, not a score: CHECKED HER PAGE goes from "just now" to "forgot". The payoff is a word, not a number.
2. The ending as a choice: "Tonight is day one. Or one more night." It puts the decision on the viewer the same night they watch.
3. A counter's label can change at the turn (SUBSCRIBERS becomes LEVEL). Reread the device; do not add a new one.
4. A plan written on paper and pinned up (MONK MODE 90 DAYS, the note "It counts before it shows") is a planted object to reread at the end and to hand to the mirror character.
5. A second turn inside the practice act: he started for the wrong reason (for her) and finishes for himself.
6. Titles can be a specific number or a tiny story ("He Sent Her 41 Texts") when a one-word title is taken or too broad.

## Patterns across all of them
1. First 5 seconds: one past-tense caption about "he" that states the pain ("He was going to start."). Or a happy before-shot that will be lost (COMFORT ZONE).
2. A device on screen from the first minute that measures the pain: a bar, bpm, a map, a calendar, notes. It gets worse in act 1, stalls at rock bottom, and changes meaning at the turn.
3. The villain is a red-glowing black silhouette. Its short lower-case lines are shown in red text and also whispered. It grows to full size at the climax and is wiped out by light.
4. The lesson is drawn, not said: a graph, a circle, a map, a quote with its author.
5. Act 1 sets up 3-5 places or moments so act 3 can replay them with the new behaviour ("The bus still left.").
6. A mirror ending: a younger kid in a red cap gets the lesson passed on, or the opening shot replays changed.
7. Title card: one or two words in caps with a red wavy underline, a quiet white truth, then a 2-3 word command in red.
8. Length 2:40 to 4:36. Chapters on YouTube use the beat names ("The window", "99", "The stage").
9. Colour is freer than our rule: warm exteriors (sunset park, orange track, sunrise), side characters in coloured beanies. Dark and muted until the turn, warmer after it.
10. Heavy topics end with a helpline card. Some films end with a like/subscribe/Skool end screen.
